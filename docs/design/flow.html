Decisions this design forces

Six things the mockups had to assume. Each is either an open question already recorded in the docs, or a contradiction the design surfaced. Every one changes a screen.

01Does the readiness score get a public name?

It appears on /today, on the share card, and in every check-in — so it is positioning as much as product. Mocked as an unnamed number 31 on a 0–100 scale benchmarked against people who got the target role. A named score ("Polaris Score") is more shareable and much harder to walk back.

platform.md §6 Q4 · affects C5, C6, A8

02How much student data goes on a public URL?

Mocked at the doc's default: college + branch + year + counts. No name, no exact CGPA. That makes the card safe under DPDP but noticeably less personal — and personal is what gets forwarded. The tension is real and unresolved.

platform.md §6 Q5 · affects A8

03Google only, or Google + LinkedIn?

The gate is the single highest-stakes screen in the funnel, so the number of buttons on it matters. Mocked with both, LinkedIn secondary. Note product.md §10.5: LinkedIn is worth having as auth, never as a data source — the design must not imply it will import anything.

platform.md §6 Q2 · affects B1

04UPI one-time, or card subscription?

product.md §7 leans one-time (₹999–4,999 for 12 months), which favours UPI. Mocked as UPI-first with card secondary, single payment. A subscription needs a different screen entirely — recurring mandate consent, not a one-tap UPI intent.

platform.md §6 Q3 · affects D3

05TCS backlog policy contradicts itself in the docs.

research.md §4's table says TCS NQT allows max 1 active backlog. The prose immediately under it says a student with two backlogs is "fine for TCS, dead for Wipro." Both cannot hold. Mocked per the table, which produces a better product anyway: clearing one backlog opens TCS and Virtusa, clearing both opens all seven. Graduated leverage is more motivating than a single cliff — but the underlying fact needs hand-verifying.

research.md §4 · affects A6, D2

06The 31-week countdown doesn't fit the graduation year.

A 3rd-year in August 2026 graduates in 2028, and §2.2's own timeline (summer internship at month 5, season tracker at month 14) confirms it. But "placement registration closes in 31 weeks" lands in March 2027 — too early for a 2028 batch. Mocked with grad year 2028 and the countdown relabelled to the summer internship window, which is what a 3rd-year is actually racing. If the 31 weeks is meant literally, the avatar is a final-year and the whole eligibility-repair thesis weakens.
