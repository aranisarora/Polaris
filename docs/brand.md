## 1. The idea

### 1.1 The fact the name is actually about

Polaris is not the brightest star in the sky. It sits somewhere around forty-eighth. Nearly every star people can name outshines it.

Its entire value is that **it does not move.** The whole sky wheels; Polaris holds. That is why it is the one you navigate by.

And the part that matters most here:

> **The angle of Polaris above your horizon equals your latitude.** You measure the fixed star, and what you learn is *where you are.*

That is the eligibility ledger, exactly. We hold still — published cutoffs, the real VTU calendar, the actual recruiter roster — and by measuring against something that does not move, a student finally gets a position. Not encouragement. A position.

From Bangalore, at roughly 13°N, Polaris sits low but visible. It is their star too, close to the horizon.

### 1.2 The brand idea, in one line

> **Polaris is the fixed point. You take your bearing from it.**

Everything else follows. We are not the loudest thing in a student's life — their WhatsApp group is louder, YouTube is louder, their uncle's advice is louder. We are the thing that is still saying the same true number tomorrow.

### 1.3 The line

**Know where you stand.**

Two meanings, both ours. *Position* — the navigational one. *Candour* — the one that means somebody finally told you. It never explains the metaphor, which is why it works.

| Alternates | Use |
|---|---|
| **Every number traces to a source.** | Trust line. Already live on the landing screen in the flow board. Keep it |
| **Fixed points. Honest arithmetic.** | Investor and TPO surfaces |
| **Nine doors are open.** | Not a tagline — a product moment. Never demote it to marketing |

**Never** "navigate your career journey," "your north star for placements," or any sentence that explains what Polaris means. A brand that annotates its own metaphor has lost the argument.

### 1.4 What we are not

`product.md` §4 sets positioning against competitors. This is positioning against **aesthetics** — the visual languages that would misfile us.

| Not this | Because |
|---|---|
| **Night-sky gradient, starfield, twinkle** | The obvious read of the name, therefore the worthless one. It also costs payload against a hard < 3s ledger budget (`platform.md` §1.5) for zero information |
| **The four-point sparkle glyph** | It is now the universal "AI feature" icon. Wearing it files us beside every wrapper — the exact accusation §4 exists to defeat. Disqualified on strategy, not taste |
| **Purple→blue SaaS gradient, glassmorphism, soft 3D** | Reads *startup*. We are asking a nineteen-year-old to believe an unwelcome number about their own life. Startups are not believed; records are |
| **"Your AI career copilot"** | Hard Rule 2 (§8) says the LLM is never the source of a fact. Leading with the model contradicts the product |
| **Rockets, confetti, streaks, badges** | Gamification says *this is a game*. The re-shock (`product.md` §9.5) is a real event and needs no decoration |
| **Stock photos of smiling students** | Every college brochure in India. Our student has learned those pictures mean nothing |
| **Hero carousel** | `platform.md` §4.1: `/` **is the tool.** Field one above the fold |

> **The visual system has one strategic job beyond looking good: it must make the wrapper accusation obviously false at a glance.** Tables, citations, tabular figures and stated provenance are what retrieved fact looks like. Prose in a chat bubble is what a wrapper looks like. `product.md` §12.2 is a design instruction as much as an architectural one.

## 2. Strategy

### 2.1 The proposition ladder

| Level | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Category** | The fixed reference point for a student who cannot tell whether they are on track |
| **Function** | Which doors are open, which are settled, and what to do this week |
| **Emotional** | The relief of a straight answer, from something with no reason to flatter you |
| **Identity** | *I am someone who knows where they stand* |

The daily suffering (§5) is **calibration** — there is no scoreboard. The brand's job is to be a scoreboard that cannot be argued with.

### 2.2 Personality

Five traits. Each states what it licenses and what it forbids — a trait with no prohibition attached is decoration.

| Trait | Which means | Which never |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** | Same number today and next month. Sources shown, dates shown, corrections logged in public | Never a number that moves because it flatters better |
| **Exact** | 63.8% against a 65% floor. 1.2%. 340 hours | Never "you may not qualify for some companies" |
| **Unsentimental** | Says the food-delivery clone is worth nothing (`product.md` §9.3) | Never cruel, never a verdict on the person. Records, not judgements |
| **On their side** | Every finding carries its fix, sized in hours. Leads with what is open | Never despair without a lever (`product.md` §10.3) |
| **Quiet** | Restraint at the emotional peak. The fact carries it | Never celebration the student did not earn |

The line between unsentimental and cruel is exactly this: **we are harsh about artefacts and never about people.** "Your three projects appear on two hundred thousand CVs" is a fact about the CV. "You have not done enough" is a verdict on a person. The first is the product; the second gets us screenshotted for the wrong reason.

### 2.3 Two audience truths that constrain everything

**1. The brand's primary surface is a screenshot in a group chat.** Acquisition is `product.md` §13.5's test — would they forward it unasked. So a screenshot is not an export feature; it is the main channel, and it must survive WhatsApp's re-encode. This produces hard rules, in §9.

**2. The brand renders on a budget Android on saturated campus wifi.** Anything that costs a second of load is a brand failure, no matter how it looks in Figma. Cheapness is not a compromise here — a brand that arrives fast *is* the fixed point behaving as advertised.

## 3. Voice

### 3.1 Principles

The flow board already settled most of this in v2/v3. Ratified here, unchanged:

1. **Headlines under seven words.** "Nine doors are open." "Seven doors just opened." "Your CV just improved itself."
2. **No hedges.** *Genuinely, actually, precisely, quite, fairly* — cut on sight.
3. **Lead with what is open.** Never "eligible for 9 of 23." Always "Nine doors are open." No number changes; the framing does. Despair produces churn (`product.md` §9.2.1)
4. **"Settled," never "permanent."** The door is still closed and the screen still says so. What changed is the instruction attached: not *this will never change* but **nothing to fix here.** Settled rows are grey so the eye lands on amber and green first
5. **Every finding carries its fix, sized in hours.** A finding without a lever is just an insult
6. **Numbers before adjectives.** "4 commits, all on one day" beats "limited GitHub activity"
7. **Second person, present tense, active.** "You are locked out of Infosys by 1.2%"
8. **Never console.** No "don't worry," no "that's okay." Consolation implies a wound; we are reading an instrument
9. **No exclamation marks. No emoji in product copy.** Emoji render inconsistently across budget Android and compress badly in a forwarded screenshot. State colour and glyph carry what emoji would (§5.3)

### 3.2 Sentence patterns we own

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Count, then consequence** | "Nine doors are open. Eligible isn't hired." |
| **The arithmetic, shown** | "6.4 now. 7.0 needs a 7.9 average across three semesters." |
| **The measurement, then the source** | "Wipro requires 0 active backlogs [Wipro 2026 criteria]. You have 2." |
| **Cost in hours, never in effort** | "Forty minutes." "You're 80 hours from standing out." |
| **Closure without instruction** | "Infosys: 63.8% against a 65% floor. Settled." |

### 3.3 Lexicon

**Say:** doors · open · within reach · settled · ledger · bearing · the arithmetic · traces to · cutoff · window · this week · hours · evidence

**Never say:** journey · unlock your potential · empower · dream job · hustle · grind · supercharge · revolutionise · game-changing · seamless · AI-powered · rockstar · ninja · "level up"

**Also never:** *just, simply, easy.* The work is not easy and pretending otherwise insults someone who has already failed at it once.

**Careful with "personalised."** We are not personalised, we are **specific.** Personalisation is a feature every edtech app claims. Specificity is a claim only we can survive being checked on.

### 3.4 Worked rewrites

| Slop | Polaris |
|---|---|
| "Unlock your dream career with AI-powered insights!" | "See which companies you can walk into today." |
| "You may want to consider improving your CGPA." | "6.4 now. 7.0 needs a 7.9 average across three semesters." |
| "Oops! It looks like you're not eligible for this one 😔" | "Infosys: 63.8% against a 65% floor. Settled." |
| "Great job! You're crushing it! 🎉" | "Seven doors just opened." |
| "Our AI analysed your profile and found some areas for growth." | "Your three projects appear on roughly 200,000 CVs. Your differentiating signal is: none." |

## 4. The aesthetic — **Almanac**

### 4.1 The thesis

A nautical almanac is the book you open with a sextant in your other hand. Tables of positions, printed once a year, no argument in them. It does not motivate you. It tells you where things are so you can work out where *you* are.

> **Almanac, not app. A record, not a feed. Instruments, not illustration.**

Concretely: paper rather than canvas. Ruled tables rather than cards floating in space. Figures set in a face that lines up in columns. Ink, one navigational blue, and three working state colours that mean something. Generous rules, tight rhythm, nothing that glows.

This is not nostalgia and there is no parchment, no compass rose, no wax seal. Take the *discipline* of printed reference material — hierarchy through weight and rule rather than through colour and shadow — and set it at 2026 fidelity.

The flow board's author already had this instinct: system serif for verdicts, monospace for data, `#223FA6` ink-blue, `#FBFBFC` paper, no gradients anywhere. §5 and §6 formalise it rather than replacing it.

### 4.2 References

Nautical almanacs and sight-reduction tables · Swiss railway timetables · Ordnance Survey sheets · Tufte's small multiples · the audited financial statement · Braun instrument faces · a physics lab notebook · the *FT* and *Economist* data pages.

Not: dashboard dribbble, edtech, fintech neobank, "AI product," crypto.

### 4.3 Roads not taken

| Direction | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Celestial / observatory** — deep navy, star charts, constellation lines | The literal read of the name. Beautiful for one screen, then it fights every table in the product. And navy-and-gold is a dead heat with every fintech |
| **Warm coaching** — rounded sans, soft peach, friendly illustration | Directly contradicts `product.md` §9.3. You cannot say "your differentiating signal is none" in a rounded typeface with a cartoon mascot. The form would call the content a lie |
| **Brutalist / terminal** — all mono, hard black, ASCII rules | Correct instincts, wrong audience. Reads as a hacker toy to a nineteen-year-old choosing whether to trust us with their marks, and it screenshots badly at thumbnail |
| **Indian vernacular** — block-print motifs, saffron/green | Our student is not looking for cultural affirmation; they are looking for an answer about a GCC job. Would read as a government scheme |

## 5. Colour

### 5.1 The system

Adopted from `design/flow.html`'s `--p-*` tokens, with two corrections in §5.4. Names are new; the product surface stays **fixed light** — a light, paper-like surface reads as a document rather than an app, and it is the only reliable substrate for a screenshot that will be viewed on an unknown phone at unknown brightness.

```css
:root {
  /* Paper */
  --p-paper:    #FBFBFC;  /* the surface everything sits on */
  --p-surface:  #FFFFFF;  /* raised: cards, sheets, the share frame */

  /* Ink */
  --p-ink:      #10141C;  /* verdicts, figures that matter        17.8:1 AAA */
  --p-ink-2:    #394152;  /* body, ledes                           9.9:1 AAA */
  --p-muted:    #626A80;  /* meta, labels, source tags             5.2:1 AA  */
  --p-faint:    #949CAD;  /* NON-TEXT ONLY — ticks, empty states, rules */

  /* Rules */
  --p-line:     #E4E7ED;  /* hairline between rows */
  --p-line-2:   #C9CFDA;  /* structural: table edges, frame borders */
  --p-line-3:   #868FA4;  /* interactive borders — inputs, chips    3.1:1    */

  /* Meridian — the one accent. Action, links, focus. Never decoration. */
  --p-accent:   #223FA6;  /*                                        8.7:1 AAA */
  --p-accent-w: #EBEFFB;

  /* The three states. Load-bearing — never decorative. */
  --p-open:     #14654A;  --p-open-w: #E8F3EE;  /* Open now      6.8:1 AA */
  --p-fix:      #A05A00;  --p-fix-w:  #FCF3E5;  /* Within reach  5.1:1 AA */
  --p-settled:  #5A6273;  --p-settled-w: #F0F2F5; /* Settled     5.9:1 AA */
}
```

Ratios are measured against `--p-paper`, not assumed. Every text token above passes WCAG AA at body size; `--p-ink` and `--p-ink-2` clear AAA.

### 5.2 The three states are the brand

Most products pick a palette and then find things to colour. Polaris is the reverse: **the palette is a state machine, and it is the most distinctive thing we own visually.** Three colours, three futures for a door.

| State | Colour | Says | Instruction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open now** | Green `#14654A` | Eligible today | Convert it |
| **Within reach** | Amber `#A05A00` | Closed, and fixable | Here is the lever, in hours |
| **Settled** | Grey `#5A6273` | Closed, nothing to fix | *No instruction.* That is the point |

Both closed states are desaturated relative to green on purpose — the eye must land on what is open and what is winnable before it finds what is lost. Grey for settled is a **product** decision (v2 note) that the palette exists to serve.

Green is deep and unsaturated — nearer a ledger's ink than a "success" toast. Amber is umber, not warning-yellow. Nothing in this palette is a notification colour.

### 5.3 Rules

1. **Never encode state in colour alone.** Every state carries a glyph *and* a word: `●` Open now, `◐` Within reach, `○` Settled. Roughly 8% of men have a colour vision deficiency and our audience is majority male engineering students — a red/amber/green product that leans on hue is broken for a meaningful slice of a lecture hall. This is also what makes a compressed screenshot survive.
2. **Meridian blue is for action only.** Links, primary buttons, focus rings. The moment it becomes a decorative brand wash, the student cannot tell what is tappable.
3. **One accent, full stop.** No secondary brand colour. Where a fourth colour seems needed, the answer is a rule, a weight change, or more space.
4. **Washes are backgrounds, never text.** `--p-*-w` tints only.
5. **No gradients. No coloured shadows. No glow.** Elevation is a hairline and, at most, a shadow at ≤ 8% opacity.

### 5.4 Three corrections to the flow board's palette

Measured, not eyeballed. Every ratio below was computed against the actual token values, not estimated.

| Token | Was | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--p-muted` | `#6B7488` → `#666E85` | **`#626A80`** | Corrected twice. See the audit below — the first correction verified two of the four washes and passed; the two it did not check both fail |
| `--p-faint` | text colour | **non-text only** | `#949CAD` is **2.67:1** — well under AA. In the flow board it currently sets `23 companies · 4 colleges · updated 2 Aug 2026`, which is *trust-bearing* text and the first thing lost to JPEG compression. There is no room to darken it without colliding with muted, so the honest resolution is that the palette's text floor is `--p-muted`, and faint is for rules, tick marks and empty states. **Move that line to `--p-muted`** |
| `--p-line-3` | *(new)* | **`#868FA4`** | WCAG 1.4.11 wants 3:1 on the boundary of an interactive component. `--p-line-2` at 1.51:1 is fine for a table rule but too faint for an input border. `#868FA4` reaches 3.13:1 and keeps the cool hue |

#### The muted-on-wash audit

`--p-muted` carries the source tag (§8.3), and a source tag sits inside a *tinted row* — which means it must clear AA on all six surfaces it can land on, not just on paper. Full matrix:

| Value | paper | surface | accent-w | open-w | fix-w | settled-w | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#6B7488` *(flow board, live)* | 4.53 | 4.69 | 4.08 ❌ | 4.13 ❌ | 4.26 ❌ | 4.18 ❌ | **4.08** |
| `#666E85` *(first correction)* | 4.91 | 5.08 | 4.42 ❌ | 4.47 ❌ | 4.62 | 4.53 | **4.42** |
| **`#626A80`** *(adopted)* | 5.22 | 5.39 | **4.69** | **4.75** | **4.90** | **4.81** | **4.69** |

The first correction checked muted against the *settled* and *reach* washes — the two it names — and both pass. It did not check the **accent wash** or the **open wash**, and both fail. `#626A80` clears AA on all six with margin to spare.

> **The rule this produces:** a token that appears on more than one background is not verified until it has been measured on *every* background it can appear on. Paper is not the worst case; the washes are.

`#626A80` sits closer in luminance to `--p-settled` (`#5A6273`) than the old value did. That is acceptable because settled is never distinguished by hue alone — it always carries the `○` glyph and the word (§5.3 rule 1). If the two ever read as the same colour in practice, the fix is weight and glyph, not a fourth grey.

## 6. Typography

### 6.1 Three voices, one rule

| Voice | Face | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **Verdict** | Display serif | The single declarative sentence per screen. Nothing else |
| **Body** | System sans | Ledes, labels, buttons, chrome. Deliberately neutral |
| **Figure** | Monospace, tabular | Every number, cutoff, date, count and source tag |

The rule that makes this a system rather than three fonts:

> **If it came from the database, it is set in the Figure face.** If the model wrote it, it is Body. If it is the one thing the screen exists to say, it is Verdict.

So typography encodes provenance. A student can *see* which parts are retrieved fact and which are prose — §8's Hard Rule 2 made visible, and the cheapest possible answer to "isn't this just ChatGPT?"

Verdict is rationed. One per screen. A serif used for every heading is a magazine; a serif used once is a statement.

### 6.2 Faces, against the performance budget

**Phase 0 ships zero webfonts on the ledger path.** The `/check → /ledger` route is the most valuable and most fragile moment in the funnel (< 3s on mid-range Android, 4G) and a font swap there is a visible flash for no gain. Keep the flow board's system stacks:

```css
--font-display: "Iowan Old Style", Charter, Cambria, Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif;
--font-ui:      system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI Variable Text", "Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif;
--font-data:    ui-monospace, "Cascadia Mono", "SF Mono", "Roboto Mono", Consolas, monospace;
```

Most of our users land on Roboto and a Noto or Droid serif. That is acceptable: **the system's character lives in the rationing, the rules and the figures, not in owning a typeface.**

When a face is affordable — marketing pages, the wordmark, `/for-colleges`, the TPO PDF — subset it and load it *only there*:

| Slot | Recommendation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Newsreader** or **Instrument Serif** | Both OFL. Editorial rather than literary; Instrument Serif has more edge at large sizes |
| Figure | **IBM Plex Mono** | Honest and technical without the developer-tool costume. True tabular figures |
| Body | Leave as system-ui | If ever forced: **Public Sans** or **IBM Plex Sans**. Not Inter — it is the default of the thing we are trying not to look like |

### 6.3 Scale

From the flow board, ratified. Verdict tracks at `-0.018em`, line-height 1.16, `text-wrap: balance`.

| Token | Size | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `v-xl` | 30px | The shock. Ledger, audit, re-shock |
| `v-lg` | 24px | Screen verdict |
| `v-md` | 19px | Section verdict |
| lede | 14px / 1.55 | One sentence. Ideally zero |
| body | 14px | Default |
| tiny | 12px | Meta. **The floor for content** — source tags, dates, counts, context lines |
| eyebrow | 10.5px, `.1em`, upper, mono | **Labels only.** See the carve-out below |

> **The 10.5px carve-out.** Uppercase mono tracked at `.1em` stays legible below the 12px floor where sentence-case would not, so *labels* may sit at 10.5px — "OPEN NOW", "YOUR TRAJECTORY", "SOMEONE LIKE YOU". A label names a region the reader is already looking at. The moment an element carries information that appears nowhere else — a source tag, a date, a count — it is content, and content is 12px. Building the demo (`design/brand-demo.html`) is what surfaced this: the source tag had been drawn at 11px, which is exactly the element §8.3 calls close to the most important in the system.

### 6.4 Numerals

1. **`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on every figure.** Columns must align; a jittering readiness score reads as an animation rather than a measurement.
2. **Never round a cutoff.** 63.8%, not "about 64%." The precision *is* the credibility.
3. **Units close and small.** `340` at figure weight, `hours` at muted.
4. **Indian numbering where money appears.** ₹1,499 · 12 LPA · lakh. Never `$` and never `1,499.00`.
5. **A figure never appears without its provenance** when it came from the registry. See §7.3.

## 7. The marks

### 7.1 Primary — **The Fix**

A sight mark, not a star: a fixed point with four ticks, the northern one longer and breaking out further than the rest. A surveyor's benchmark, a reticle, a position plotted on a chart.

The asymmetric north tick is the whole design. It encodes the meridian, it makes the mark legibly *drawn* rather than defaulted, and — critically — it keeps us clear of the four-point sparkle now stamped on every AI feature in the world (§1.4).

```svg
<svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" width="24" height="24" fill="none"
     stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.6" stroke-linecap="round"
     role="img" aria-label="Polaris">
  <circle cx="12" cy="12" r="2.4" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/>
  <path d="M12 1.5V7"/>      <!-- meridian: longest, breaks the ring -->
  <path d="M12 17v3.5"/>
  <path d="M3.5 12H7"/>
  <path d="M17 12h3.5"/>
</svg>
```

Renders in `currentColor` — ink on paper, paper on Meridian, never two-tone.

### 7.2 Secondary — **The Ledger**

Four bars at uneven heights, echoing the 23-tick counter. Already specified as the Ledger tab icon in the flow board's v3 note; promoted here to the app icon and favicon, because at 32px a bar chart survives where a reticle turns to mush.

```svg
<svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" width="24" height="24" fill="currentColor"
     role="img" aria-label="Ledger">
  <rect x="3"    y="9"  width="3" height="12" rx="1.5"/>
  <rect x="8.5"  y="5"  width="3" height="16" rx="1.5"/>
  <rect x="14"   y="12" width="3" height="9"  rx="1.5"/>
  <rect x="19.5" y="15" width="3" height="6"  rx="1.5"/>
</svg>
```

### 7.3 Wordmark

**Polaris** — Display face, weight 500, tracking `-0.018em`, sentence case. Never all-caps, never lowercase, never letterspaced open.

The flow board's header lockup is already correct and becomes the standard: wordmark in Display, with an optional mono eyebrow beside or beneath it at 10.5px / `.1em` / uppercase / muted.

```
Polaris   FOR VTU 3RD-YEARS · BENGALURU
```

**Lockups:** horizontal (mark + wordmark, gap = 0.6× cap height, baselines aligned) · stacked (mark centred above, gap = 1× cap height) · wordmark alone — the default in-product · mark alone for favicon, app icon and the share-card corner.

### 7.4 Rules

- **Clear space:** the length of the north tick on every side. Nothing enters it.
- **Minimums:** mark 16px (at 16px drop the side ticks — dot and meridian only); wordmark 72px wide.
- **Colour:** Ink, Meridian, or paper knocked out of Meridian. Nothing else.
- **Misuse:** do not rotate · do not add rays, glow or a starburst · do not gradient the dot · do not place on photography · do not outline the wordmark · do not set the wordmark in the Body face · never animate the mark as a twinkle.

## 8. Signature components

A brand needs one thing that is unmistakably its own before it needs a logo. Ours is not the mark — it is the row.

### 8.1 The ruled row

The atom of the product and the thing that gets screenshotted.

```
● Open now      TCS NQT          6.4 ≥ 6.0 · 0 backlogs      [TCS 2026 criteria]
◐ Within reach  Wipro Elite      2 active backlogs → 0       [Wipro 2026 criteria]
○ Settled       Infosys          12th 63.8% vs 65%           [Infosys 2026 criteria]
```

State glyph and label at the left, entity in Body, **the arithmetic in Figures**, source tag at the right. Hairline `--p-line` between rows, no card, no shadow. Repeated twenty-three times, ruled and aligned, this *is* the brand — and no wrapper can produce it, because it needs a registry.

### 8.2 The tick counter

Twenty-three marks, nine filled. Appears on `/today`, the share card and, as four bars, in the nav. It is the readiness and eligibility idea in a form that is legible at 12px and at thumbnail size in a group chat. Never a donut chart. Never a percentage ring.

### 8.3 The source tag

`[Wipro 2026 criteria]` — mono, muted, bracketed, **12px minimum**, always adjacent to the figure it justifies.

This is the smallest element in the system and close to the most important. It is `product.md` §12.2 made visible, it is what makes an unwelcome number survive being doubted, and it is the single clearest visual difference between us and a chat answer. **A retrieved figure never ships without one.**

### 8.4 The share card — `/r/[slug]`

The most-seen brand surface we will ever have, and the one an art director never sees. Designed under §9's rules.

Contains: the count as the hero (`9 / 23`) · the tick counter · at most three ruled rows, mixed states · college + branch + year, no name, no exact CGPA (`platform.md` §6 Q5) · the wordmark, bottom-left, always · a stated date.

Constraints: readable at 240px wide · legible cropped to the top half · no text under 12px · nothing essential in the outer 8% · flat colour only, since gradients band badly under WhatsApp's re-encode.

## 9. Designed for the screenshot

§2.3 makes this the primary channel, so it gets rules rather than good intentions. Every one of these is a consequence of an image being re-encoded, resized, and viewed on a stranger's phone.

1. **12px is the floor for content.** The only exception is the 10.5px uppercase mono label (§6.3) — and a label never carries information that appears nowhere else.
2. **Nothing meaningful at `--p-faint`.** Low-contrast grey is the first casualty of JPEG.
3. **The wordmark is in frame on every shareable surface** — ledger, audit, re-shock, share card. An unattributed screenshot is free reach we did not collect.
4. **Nothing essential in the outer 8%.** People crop badly and thumbnails clip.
5. **State reads without colour** (§5.3) — glyph and word, so it survives a screenshot of a screenshot.
6. **Flat fills only.** Gradients band; hairlines below 1px vanish.
7. **A date on every artefact.** "Updated 2 Aug 2026" is what makes a forwarded image credible in November.

> Design review question for any new surface: *if someone crops this to a square and sends it to three friends, does it still make the argument — and can they tell who made it?*

## 10. Motion

### 10.1 The position

Restraint is the position, not a budget constraint. **A record does not animate.** But the app *around* the record is a habit surface returned to weekly, and it must feel alive under the thumb. Those are two different jobs, and conflating them is how products like this end up either lifeless or unserious. §10.4 draws the line.

### 10.2 Tokens

```css
:root {
  --m-state:  90ms;  --e-state:  linear;                        /* glyph swap, check, focus */
  --m-micro: 120ms;  --e-out:    cubic-bezier(.2, 0, 0, 1);     /* press, chip, hover      */
  --m-move:  200ms;                                              /* tab commit, push, sheet */
  --m-weight:260ms;                                              /* row state, wash tint    */
  --m-spring:320ms;  --e-spring: cubic-bezier(.34, 1.16, .64, 1);/* gesture cancel ONLY     */
}
```

Three rules govern all of it:

1. **Nothing eases in.** Every curve starts fast and settles. `ease-in` reads as the interface *deciding*; ease-out reads as the interface having already decided. We are never deciding — we are reporting.
2. **Overshoot exists in exactly one place:** a cancelled gesture springing back. `--e-spring` appears nowhere else. Bounce anywhere else reads as playful, and the content is not.
3. **Under the finger, motion is linear and 1:1.** Easing applies only after release. This single rule is most of the difference between "native" and "web trying to be native."

### 10.3 What never moves

- **The mark.** It does not move — that is the entire idea (§7.4).
- **Figures on the ledger.** A cutoff that counts up reads as a slot machine. The number is a *measurement*; measurements are already true when you look at them.
- **Source tags.** They appear with their figure or not at all.
- **State glyphs.** They swap at `--m-state`. They do not morph, spin, or slide.
- **Row order.** Rows never re-sort under the reader. A door that changes state changes **in place** — the whole point is that the student watches *that specific row* flip.

Two carve-outs, both earned:

| Carve-out | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **The countdown** (`platform.md` §3.1) | Weeks, windows and hours count up during the eligibility resolve, then hold. Dead wait becomes framing |
| **Altitude at the re-shock** | Counts once, decelerating. Never on load, never on return visits |

Both run on tabular figures at fixed width so nothing reflows mid-count. A number that changes width while counting is worse than no animation at all.

### 10.4 The two zones

This is the line the whole motion system hangs on.

| | **The Record** | **The App** |
|---|---|---|
| Surfaces | `/`, `/check`, the public ledger, `/r/[slug]`, `/companies/*`, the TPO PDF | `/today`, `/ledger`, `/roadmap`, `/signal`, `/check-in` |
| Motion | State changes only. No gesture layer | Full gesture layer (§11) |
| Client JS | Effectively none. Server-rendered | Client shell, adjacent panes prefetched |
| Governed by | `platform.md` §1.5 — interactive < 3s on mid-range Android, 4G | Feel, not first paint |

A student meets the Record first and does not see the App until after the gate (`platform.md` §3.2). That ordering is deliberate:

> **The credibility artefact must not behave like software.** A page that swipes, springs and transitions is a *product*. A page that is simply, instantly there is a *record*. We need to be believed before we need to be enjoyed.

### 10.5 The re-shock — the one choreographed moment

`product.md` §7 identifies this as peak willingness to pay. It is the only sequence in the product that gets a beat sheet.

Seven doors moving **Within reach → Open now** after a cleared backlog:

| Beat | At | What |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0ms | **A held pause.** Nothing moves. The screen the student was already looking at |
| 1 | 200ms | The counter begins. `9 → 16`, decelerating over 900ms |
| 2 | 260ms | Rows begin flipping, **staggered 60ms**, in ledger order, top to bottom. Each row: glyph `◐→●` at `--m-state`, label crossfade, wash tint amber→green over `--m-weight` |
| 3 | ~1.4s | The verdict sets: **"Seven doors just opened."** Fade with a 2px rise, 200ms |
| 4 | — | **Stillness.** The share control was already there and does not pop in |

Total ≈ 1.6s, then nothing. No confetti, no sound, no burst, no badge. This is one of the more significant things to happen to this student all semester and treating it like a mobile game would cheapen it. **The fact carries it.**

The upgrade prompt does *not* ride this animation. It appears after the stillness, quietly, in its own time. Selling into the peak is the one move that would make the moment feel manufactured.

### 10.6 Reduced motion

The contract, stated so it cannot be misread:

> **`prefers-reduced-motion` removes movement. It never removes information.**

Crossfades become instant swaps. Counting jumps to the final figure. Staggers collapse to simultaneous. The re-shock still turns seven rows green and still says *"Seven doors just opened."* A student who has motion turned off learns exactly as much, in less time.

Gesture navigation stays available under reduced motion — a swipe still changes tabs, it just arrives without the transition. Removing the gesture would remove function, not movement.

## 11. The native feel

`platform.md` §1 settles the form factor: responsive web, PWA at Phase 1, never native. That decision is about *distribution*. It says nothing about how the thing should feel once a student is inside it, and the honest answer is that the app half should feel like an app.

### 11.1 What "native" actually means here

Not iOS chrome. Not a bottom bar with a blur. Four properties, and every one is achievable on the web:

1. **Response precedes the network.** A tap changes the screen now and reconciles later. Nothing waits on a round trip to acknowledge that it was touched.
2. **Gestures are continuous and reversible.** What you drag follows your finger exactly, and you can change your mind halfway through.
3. **Space is consistent.** Screens hold fixed positions relative to each other. Back reverses precisely what forward did.
4. **Chrome never reflows.** The tab bar and header do not move when content loads, when the keyboard opens, or when a number changes width.

Miss any one and it reads as a website. Hit all four and almost nobody asks whether it was installed.

### 11.2 The tab model

**Four tabs.** Four is what a thumb reaches without a stretch, and it is the ceiling before labels start abbreviating.

| Tab | Route | Holds | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Today** | `/today` | Max 3 items, Altitude, countdown | What now |
| **Ledger** | `/ledger` | 23 ruled rows, `/companies/[slug]` pushes from here | Where I stand |
| **Roadmap** | `/roadmap` | The whole plan, `/roadmap/[task]` pushes from here | Where I'm going |
| **Signal** | `/signal` | Audit, reach set, connections, CV *(Phase 1)* | What I'm worth |

Left to right, the tabs read as a sentence: **what now → where I stand → where I'm going → what I'm worth.** Swipe order and narrative order are the same thing, which is why nobody will need to learn it.

Three placements that follow:

- **Settings** is a header affordance, not a tab. It is visited twice a year and does not deserve a quarter of the thumb zone.
- **`/check-in`** is a **sheet**, not a tab (§11.5). It is a sub-30-second task, it is entered from a push notification or a deep link, and it must return the student to wherever they were.
- **`/season`** (Phase 2) does not become a fifth tab. During placement season it **takes Today's slot**, because in season it *is* the today surface. Never five tabs.

**"Signal" over "Profile."** Profile is what every app calls it. Signal is already the product's own word — *"your differentiating signal is: none"* (`product.md` §9.3) — and the tab is where a student goes to find out what they are worth to a recruiter. `product.md` §13.5 wants insider vocabulary; this is free insider vocabulary.

### 11.3 Swipe

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| Range | **Adjacent tabs only.** No skipping, no wrap-around from Signal back to Today |
| Tracking | 1:1 with the finger while down. Linear, no easing (§10.2 rule 3) |
| Resistance | Rubber-band at the first and last tab — 0.35 multiplier, so it moves but obviously refuses |
| Commit | Displacement ≥ **35%** of viewport width **or** velocity ≥ **0.35 px/ms**. Either one, not both |
| Cancel | Snap back at `--m-spring` — the only overshoot in the system |
| Release | Settle at `--m-move` with `--e-out` |

**The indicator scrubs.** The tab bar's active indicator moves *continuously with the drag*, not on commit. It is a scrubber, not a state change. This is the single detail most responsible for a swipe feeling native, and the one most often skipped.

**Axis lock.** Within the first 10px of movement, if `|dx| > |dy| × 1.2` the gesture is horizontal and vertical scroll is suppressed for its duration; otherwise it is vertical and horizontal is suppressed. Decide once, never re-evaluate mid-gesture. An interface that changes its mind about which axis you are on feels broken in a way users cannot articulate but always notice.

**Each tab owns its scroll position.** Returning to Ledger returns you to the row you were reading. This is quietly the biggest "feels native" tell in the entire list — every native app does it and almost no website does.

> **Hard constraint: no horizontally scrollable content inside a swipe pane, ever.** A wide table inside a horizontal gesture is an unresolvable conflict. Ledger rows must **wrap or truncate, never scroll sideways** — which is already the right call for §8.1, since a row whose arithmetic is off-screen cannot be screenshotted. Likewise **no nested horizontal gestures**: Ledger's state filters are chips that filter in place, never sub-tabs.

### 11.4 Push, pop, and the back button

- Detail screens **push from the right** at `--m-move`; the parent slides left 25% and dims 8%. Pop reverses exactly.
- **Interactive edge-back:** a drag from the left 20px follows the finger and can be abandoned. Same commit thresholds as §11.3.
- **Every push is a real history entry.** Android's system back gesture is the back button for most of our users, and it must pop the detail rather than exiting the app.
- **Every tab is a real `<a href>` to a real URL.** The gesture layer is progressive enhancement over working links. If the JS fails, has not loaded, or is disabled, tapping a tab does a normal navigation and the product still works. This is not a nicety — the acquisition path depends on URLs (`platform.md` §1.2), and a tab bar that only works with JS is a tab bar that breaks on campus wifi.

### 11.5 Sheets

`/check-in` and any short modal task present as a **bottom sheet**: 92% max height, one detent, drag-to-dismiss with the same velocity threshold, backdrop dimming to 32% ink.

The sheet exists so the check-in never loses the student's place. They arrive from a push notification, answer in under thirty seconds (`product.md` §11.3), and are returned to exactly where they were. A check-in that navigates away is a check-in that costs a re-orientation every week.

### 11.6 The header

**No collapsing header.** Collapsing headers reflow content, and §11.1 rule 4 forbids that.

Instead: the header is a fixed hairline bar. The **Verdict** line scrolls away naturally with the content, and when it crosses the header the screen's short title **cross-fades in** at `--m-micro`. One `IntersectionObserver`, no layout thrash, and it produces the large-title-to-small-title behaviour people read as native without any of its cost.

### 11.7 Touch, keyboard, safe area

| Concern | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Targets** | 44px minimum, 48px preferred. Primary actions in the bottom third — thumb zone, not the top-right corner |
| **Hover** | Every hover style behind `@media (hover: hover)`. Sticky hover on touch is the loudest "this is a website" tell there is |
| **Safe area** | Tab bar padded by `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`. Non-negotiable on iPhone home-indicator and Android gesture nav |
| **Overscroll** | `overscroll-behavior-y: contain` on panes. Browser pull-to-refresh mid-swipe is a lost gesture and a lost place. No custom pull-to-refresh in Phase 0 — nothing updates in real time |
| **Tap highlight** | `-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent`, replaced with a real 90ms press state. The default grey box is unmistakably a web page |
| **Viewport** | `interactive-widget=resizes-content` so the keyboard resizes rather than covers |
| **Text size** | Inputs at 16px minimum. Below that, iOS Safari zooms on focus and the layout is destroyed |

**The onboarding keyboard.** `platform.md` §3.1 asks for seven fields in under 90 seconds. Getting the keyboard right is most of that budget:

| Field | Control | `inputmode` | `enterkeyhint` |
|---|---|---|---|
| College | Searchable **sheet**, never a `<select>` or combobox | — | — |
| Branch · Grad year · Target | **Chips** | — | — |
| CGPA | text | `decimal` | `next` |
| Active backlogs | text | `numeric` | `next` |
| 10th % · 12th % | text | `decimal` | `next` / `done` |

Four of the eight inputs raise no keyboard at all. The four numeric fields sit **together on one screen** so a single numeric keypad presents once and stays up — focus moves between them without a blur, so the keyboard never dismisses and re-presents. A keyboard that flaps between fields is the difference between 45 seconds and 90.

Use `type="text"` with `inputmode`, never `type="number"` — number spinners are ugly on desktop and a scroll wheel over a focused number input silently changes a student's CGPA.

**Haptics** (`navigator.vibrate`, Android only — iOS Safari does not support it): reserved for **task completion** and **the re-shock**. 10ms, once. Never on tab change, never on scroll, never on a tap that only navigated. A haptic means *something changed in your record.* If it fires on navigation it means nothing.

### 11.8 Responsive — three breakpoints

Three, not five. The same DOM at every width — never a separate mobile build.

| | **Phone** < 600px | **Tablet** 600–1024px | **Desktop** > 1024px |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nav | Bottom tab bar | Bottom tab bar | **Persistent left rail**, no tab bar |
| Swipe | On | On | Off — a trackpad swipe conflicts with browser back |
| Ledger | Single column, stacked rows | Single column, wider gutters | Two-pane: rows left, company detail right |
| Roadmap | This week only, plan behind a tap | This week + plan | Full two-semester plan visible at once |
| Verdict | 30px | 30px | 36px |

`platform.md` §1.5 makes desktop first-class for `/cv` and `/roadmap` — deep work is not a phone task. That is what the two-pane layouts are for. **Mobile-first is not mobile-only**, and the desktop layout is not the phone layout stretched.

### 11.9 The seven tells

A build checklist. Each of these is individually small and collectively the whole difference.

1. Tab scroll positions are preserved.
2. The tab indicator scrubs with the drag, not on commit.
3. Gestures are interruptible and reversible.
4. Android system back pops the detail, not the app.
5. No sticky hover, no grey tap-highlight box.
6. The keyboard presents once during onboarding and does not flap.
7. Nothing reflows when a number changes or the keyboard opens.

## 12. Atmosphere

§1.4 rejects the night-sky gradient and §5.3 rule 5 says no gradients. Both stand. But "no gradient" is a rule about *the Record*, and applying it to every surface leaves the marketing pages as untextured white boxes — which does not read as restraint, it reads as unfinished.

The resolution is the same two-zone split as §10.4.

### 12.1 The rule

> **Atmosphere is permitted where the reader is being approached. It is forbidden where the reader is being told a number.**

| Zone | Surfaces | Atmosphere |
|---|---|---|
| **Approach** | `/` above the fold, the countdown loading state, `/for-colleges`, `/pricing`, auth, empty states, 404 | Permitted, under §12.2–12.4 |
| **Record** | Ledger rows, audit, roadmap, `/r/[slug]`, TPO PDF, anything screenshottable | **None.** Flat paper, hairlines, nothing behind the figures |

A background behind a row of arithmetic makes the arithmetic look art-directed. That is the one thing it must never look.

### 12.2 The graticule — the primary background

Not a gradient. A **chart grid**: the faint ruled graticule of a navigational chart or a sheet of plotting paper.

```css
--p-graticule:
  repeating-linear-gradient(0deg,  var(--p-line) 0 1px, transparent 1px 24px),
  repeating-linear-gradient(90deg, var(--p-line) 0 1px, transparent 1px 24px);
/* applied at 40% opacity, with a 1px/120px heavier rule in --p-line-2 for the fifth line */
```

It earns its place on every axis:

- **It cannot band.** Hard stops, no interpolation — the failure mode that rules out real gradients does not apply.
- **It costs nothing.** No image request, no payload, tiles infinitely, resolution-independent.
- **It is the metaphor, not a decoration of it.** A measurement surface, which is what §1.1 says we are.
- **It survives compression**, though it should still never sit behind a screenshottable row.

Fades to transparent within 120px of any content block, so text never sits on ruling.

### 12.3 The declination arc

One faint arc — a segment of a circle whose centre is off-canvas — bleeding out of the top-right of `/` and the `/for-colleges` hero. Hairline, `--p-line-2`, 8% opacity, pure SVG, under 400 bytes.

It is a single frame of a star trail: everything wheels, and the centre it wheels around is off the edge of the page. It reads as structure rather than ornament, and it never repeats anywhere else in the product. **One arc, two surfaces, never animated.**

### 12.4 Gradients — the exact carve-out

Three permitted forms. Everything else remains forbidden.

| Permitted | Spec | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard-stop repeating** | The graticule (§12.2) | Approach surfaces |
| **Horizon wash** | Vertical, `--p-paper` → `#F4F6FA`, ≤ 4% luminance range, **with a 2% noise overlay** to defeat banding | `/` and `/for-colleges` full-page background only |
| **Scrim** | Ink 0 → 55% over an image, for text legibility | Only over §13 imagery |

Still forbidden, everywhere, permanently: coloured gradients, brand-colour washes, radial glows, coloured shadows, glassmorphism, mesh gradients, anything animated.

> A subtle long-distance gradient is the *worst* case for JPEG banding — which is precisely why the horizon wash carries a mandatory noise layer and is barred from any surface that gets screenshotted. If the noise layer is ever dropped for payload, the wash goes with it.

### 12.5 Depth

Unchanged from §5.3: elevation is a hairline, and at most a shadow at ≤ 8% opacity. Sheets (§11.5) are the one place a real shadow is permitted, because a sheet must read as being *in front of* the page rather than part of it — `0 -2px 24px rgba(16,20,28,.10)`, and nothing more.

## 13. Imagery

The first edition's position, in what is now §14, was **none**, on the grounds that every college brochure in India is stock photography of smiling students and our reader has learned those pictures mean nothing. That reasoning is correct and survives intact for **people**.

It does not follow that the brand owns no photography. `/for-colleges` and the TPO PDF are sold to a fifty-year-old placement officer on the strength of looking expensive, and an all-type page does not carry that on its own.

### 13.1 The rules

1. **Never people.** No students, no faces, no hands, no classrooms, no campuses, no graduation caps. This is the rule the whole section exists to protect.
2. **Never legible text in a generated image.** Garbage numerals in a product whose entire claim is *every number traces to a source* is an own goal that a single screenshot would make permanent.
3. **Instruments and measurement surfaces only.** Sextants, charts, ruled paper, plotted lines. Things that take a reading.
4. **Duotone in post.** Every image is reduced to `--p-ink` → `--p-paper`, with at most one `--p-accent` note. Colour photography never ships.
5. **Never on a Record surface.** Never behind a figure, a row, or a source tag.
6. **≤ 40KB, AVIF with a WebP fallback**, `loading="lazy"`, explicit `width`/`height` so nothing reflows (§11.1 rule 4).

**`/r/[slug]`'s OG image is not one of these.** It is generated from the student's own data — the count, the tick counter, three rows, the date. It is type and rules, rendered at the edge. Never an illustration, never a photograph, never a template with a photo behind it. That artefact's persuasive power *is* that it is obviously their own record.

### 13.2 The permitted set

Four images. That is the whole library, and it should stay that way.

| # | Image | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | The instrument | `/for-colleges` hero, site-wide OG fallback |
| **B** | Circumpolar star trail | TPO PDF cover, workshop title slide |
| **C** | Blank ruled stock | Section dividers, card backing, `/pricing` |
| **D** | Plotted chart | Empty states, `/roadmap` header |

Image **B** is the one celestial image the brand permits, and §1.4's rejection of "night-sky gradient, starfield, twinkle" is not being reversed to allow it. A starfield is decoration — scattered points, no argument. A star *trail* is a **measurement**: it is a photograph of everything moving around one thing that does not, which is the brand idea (§1.2) in a single frame. It is permitted for what it depicts, not for being astronomical.

### 13.3 Generation prompts

For Higgsfield. Default to **GPT Image 2** — it holds compositional constraints and negative instructions better than the photoreal-tuned models, which matters more here than photographic realism does.

### 13.4 After generation

Every image, without exception, before it ships:

1. **Duotone** to `--p-ink` → `--p-paper`. If a single accent note survives, it is `--p-accent` and nothing else.
2. **Check for hallucinated text at 100%.** Any legible numeral or letterform is an automatic reject — regenerate, do not retouch. Rule 2 has no tolerance band.
3. **Export AVIF + WebP, ≤ 40KB**, explicit dimensions.
4. **Verify a scrim (§12.4) exists** anywhere type sits over the image, at ≥ 4.5:1 against the lightest region.
5. **Confirm it never appears on a Record surface.**

An image that cannot survive being reduced to two colours was never carrying its meaning in composition, and should not ship.

## 16. Is this Polaris?

Ten questions. A no on any of them is a rewrite, not a tweak.

**The argument**

1. Could this sentence have been written without our database? *(If yes, it is a wrapper. §4)*
2. Does every retrieved figure carry its source tag?
3. Is the headline under seven words, with no hedges?
4. Does every closed door say either what fixes it, in hours, or nothing at all?
5. Cropped to a square and forwarded to three friends — does it still argue, and is the wordmark in frame?
6. Would it still look right if the news it delivered were bad? *(The form must not flatter what the content says.)*

**The build**

7. Which zone is this — Record or App? *(§10.4. If the answer is unclear, the surface is not designed yet.)*
8. Does it still work with the gesture layer dead? *(§11.4. Tabs are links; JS is enhancement.)*
9. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, does the reader learn exactly as much? *(§10.6.)*
10. Is there anything behind a figure? *(§12.1. There must not be.)*
