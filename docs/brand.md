## 1. The idea

### 1.1 The fact the name is actually about

Polaris is not the brightest star in the sky. It sits somewhere around forty-eighth. Nearly every star people can name outshines it.

Its entire value is that **it does not move.** The whole sky wheels; Polaris holds. That is why it is the one you navigate by.

And the part that matters most here:

> **The angle of Polaris above your horizon equals your latitude.** You measure the fixed star, and what you learn is *where you are.*

That is the eligibility ledger, exactly. We hold still — published cutoffs, the real VTU calendar, the actual recruiter roster — and by measuring against something that does not move, a student finally gets a position. Not encouragement. A position.

From Bangalore, at roughly 13°N, Polaris sits low but visible. It is their star too, close to the horizon.

### 1.2 The brand idea, in one line

> **Polaris is the fixed point. You take your bearing from it.**

Everything else follows. We are not the loudest thing in a student's life — their WhatsApp group is louder, YouTube is louder, their uncle's advice is louder. We are the thing that is still saying the same true number tomorrow.

### 1.3 The line

**Know where you stand.**

Two meanings, both ours. *Position* — the navigational one. *Candour* — the one that means somebody finally told you. It never explains the metaphor, which is why it works.

| Alternates | Use |
|---|---|
| **Every number traces to a source.** | Trust line. Already live on the landing screen in the flow board. Keep it |
| **Fixed points. Honest arithmetic.** | Investor and TPO surfaces |
| **Nine doors are open.** | Not a tagline — a product moment. Never demote it to marketing |

**Never** "navigate your career journey," "your north star for placements," or any sentence that explains what Polaris means. A brand that annotates its own metaphor has lost the argument.

### 1.4 What we are not

`product.md` §4 sets positioning against competitors. This is positioning against **aesthetics** — the visual languages that would misfile us.

| Not this | Because |
|---|---|
| **Night-sky gradient, starfield, twinkle** | The obvious read of the name, therefore the worthless one. It also costs payload against a hard < 3s ledger budget (`platform.md` §1.5) for zero information |
| **The four-point sparkle glyph** | It is now the universal "AI feature" icon. Wearing it files us beside every wrapper — the exact accusation §4 exists to defeat. Disqualified on strategy, not taste |
| **Purple→blue SaaS gradient, glassmorphism, soft 3D** | Reads *startup*. We are asking a nineteen-year-old to believe an unwelcome number about their own life. Startups are not believed; records are |
| **"Your AI career copilot"** | Hard Rule 2 (§8) says the LLM is never the source of a fact. Leading with the model contradicts the product |
| **Rockets, confetti, streaks, badges** | Gamification says *this is a game*. The re-shock (§9.5) is a real event and needs no decoration |
| **Stock photos of smiling students** | Every college brochure in India. Our student has learned those pictures mean nothing |
| **Hero carousel** | `platform.md` §4.1: `/` **is the tool.** Field one above the fold |

> **The visual system has one strategic job beyond looking good: it must make the wrapper accusation obviously false at a glance.** Tables, citations, tabular figures and stated provenance are what retrieved fact looks like. Prose in a chat bubble is what a wrapper looks like. §12.2 is a design instruction as much as an architectural one.

## 2. Strategy

### 2.1 The proposition ladder

| Level | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Category** | The fixed reference point for a student who cannot tell whether they are on track |
| **Function** | Which doors are open, which are settled, and what to do this week |
| **Emotional** | The relief of a straight answer, from something with no reason to flatter you |
| **Identity** | *I am someone who knows where they stand* |

The daily suffering (§5) is **calibration** — there is no scoreboard. The brand's job is to be a scoreboard that cannot be argued with.

### 2.2 Personality

Five traits. Each states what it licenses and what it forbids — a trait with no prohibition attached is decoration.

| Trait | Which means | Which never |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** | Same number today and next month. Sources shown, dates shown, corrections logged in public | Never a number that moves because it flatters better |
| **Exact** | 63.8% against a 65% floor. 1.2%. 340 hours | Never "you may not qualify for some companies" |
| **Unsentimental** | Says the food-delivery clone is worth nothing (§9.3) | Never cruel, never a verdict on the person. Records, not judgements |
| **On their side** | Every finding carries its fix, sized in hours. Leads with what is open | Never despair without a lever (§10.3) |
| **Quiet** | Restraint at the emotional peak. The fact carries it | Never celebration the student did not earn |

The line between unsentimental and cruel is exactly this: **we are harsh about artefacts and never about people.** "Your three projects appear on two hundred thousand CVs" is a fact about the CV. "You have not done enough" is a verdict on a person. The first is the product; the second gets us screenshotted for the wrong reason.

### 2.3 Two audience truths that constrain everything

**1. The brand's primary surface is a screenshot in a group chat.** Acquisition is §13.5's test — would they forward it unasked. So a screenshot is not an export feature; it is the main channel, and it must survive WhatsApp's re-encode. This produces hard rules, in §9.

**2. The brand renders on a budget Android on saturated campus wifi.** Anything that costs a second of load is a brand failure, no matter how it looks in Figma. Cheapness is not a compromise here — a brand that arrives fast *is* the fixed point behaving as advertised.

## 3. Voice

### 3.1 Principles

The flow board already settled most of this in v2/v3. Ratified here, unchanged:

1. **Headlines under seven words.** "Nine doors are open." "Seven doors just opened." "Your CV just improved itself."
2. **No hedges.** *Genuinely, actually, precisely, quite, fairly* — cut on sight.
3. **Lead with what is open.** Never "eligible for 9 of 23." Always "Nine doors are open." No number changes; the framing does. Despair produces churn (§9.2.1)
4. **"Settled," never "permanent."** The door is still closed and the screen still says so. What changed is the instruction attached: not *this will never change* but **nothing to fix here.** Settled rows are grey so the eye lands on amber and green first
5. **Every finding carries its fix, sized in hours.** A finding without a lever is just an insult
6. **Numbers before adjectives.** "4 commits, all on one day" beats "limited GitHub activity"
7. **Second person, present tense, active.** "You are locked out of Infosys by 1.2%"
8. **Never console.** No "don't worry," no "that's okay." Consolation implies a wound; we are reading an instrument
9. **No exclamation marks. No emoji in product copy.** Emoji render inconsistently across budget Android and compress badly in a forwarded screenshot. State colour and glyph carry what emoji would (§5.3)

### 3.2 Sentence patterns we own

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Count, then consequence** | "Nine doors are open. Eligible isn't hired." |
| **The arithmetic, shown** | "6.4 now. 7.0 needs a 7.9 average across three semesters." |
| **The measurement, then the source** | "Wipro requires 0 active backlogs [Wipro 2026 criteria]. You have 2." |
| **Cost in hours, never in effort** | "Forty minutes." "You're 80 hours from standing out." |
| **Closure without instruction** | "Infosys: 63.8% against a 65% floor. Settled." |

### 3.3 Lexicon

**Say:** doors · open · within reach · settled · ledger · bearing · the arithmetic · traces to · cutoff · window · this week · hours · evidence

**Never say:** journey · unlock your potential · empower · dream job · hustle · grind · supercharge · revolutionise · game-changing · seamless · AI-powered · rockstar · ninja · "level up"

**Also never:** *just, simply, easy.* The work is not easy and pretending otherwise insults someone who has already failed at it once.

**Careful with "personalised."** We are not personalised, we are **specific.** Personalisation is a feature every edtech app claims. Specificity is a claim only we can survive being checked on.

### 3.4 Worked rewrites

| Slop | Polaris |
|---|---|
| "Unlock your dream career with AI-powered insights!" | "See which companies you can walk into today." |
| "You may want to consider improving your CGPA." | "6.4 now. 7.0 needs a 7.9 average across three semesters." |
| "Oops! It looks like you're not eligible for this one 😔" | "Infosys: 63.8% against a 65% floor. Settled." |
| "Great job! You're crushing it! 🎉" | "Seven doors just opened." |
| "Our AI analysed your profile and found some areas for growth." | "Your three projects appear on roughly 200,000 CVs. Your differentiating signal is: none." |

## 4. The aesthetic — **Almanac**

### 4.1 The thesis

A nautical almanac is the book you open with a sextant in your other hand. Tables of positions, printed once a year, no argument in them. It does not motivate you. It tells you where things are so you can work out where *you* are.

> **Almanac, not app. A record, not a feed. Instruments, not illustration.**

Concretely: paper rather than canvas. Ruled tables rather than cards floating in space. Figures set in a face that lines up in columns. Ink, one navigational blue, and three working state colours that mean something. Generous rules, tight rhythm, nothing that glows.

This is not nostalgia and there is no parchment, no compass rose, no wax seal. Take the *discipline* of printed reference material — hierarchy through weight and rule rather than through colour and shadow — and set it at 2026 fidelity.

The flow board's author already had this instinct: system serif for verdicts, monospace for data, `#223FA6` ink-blue, `#FBFBFC` paper, no gradients anywhere. §5 and §6 formalise it rather than replacing it.

### 4.2 References

Nautical almanacs and sight-reduction tables · Swiss railway timetables · Ordnance Survey sheets · Tufte's small multiples · the audited financial statement · Braun instrument faces · a physics lab notebook · the *FT* and *Economist* data pages.

Not: dashboard dribbble, edtech, fintech neobank, "AI product," crypto.

### 4.3 Roads not taken

| Direction | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Celestial / observatory** — deep navy, star charts, constellation lines | The literal read of the name. Beautiful for one screen, then it fights every table in the product. And navy-and-gold is a dead heat with every fintech |
| **Warm coaching** — rounded sans, soft peach, friendly illustration | Directly contradicts §9.3. You cannot say "your differentiating signal is none" in a rounded typeface with a cartoon mascot. The form would call the content a lie |
| **Brutalist / terminal** — all mono, hard black, ASCII rules | Correct instincts, wrong audience. Reads as a hacker toy to a nineteen-year-old choosing whether to trust us with their marks, and it screenshots badly at thumbnail |
| **Indian vernacular** — block-print motifs, saffron/green | Our student is not looking for cultural affirmation; they are looking for an answer about a GCC job. Would read as a government scheme |

## 5. Colour

### 5.1 The system

Adopted from `design/flow.html`'s `--p-*` tokens, with two corrections in §5.4. Names are new; the product surface stays **fixed light** — a light, paper-like surface reads as a document rather than an app, and it is the only reliable substrate for a screenshot that will be viewed on an unknown phone at unknown brightness.

```css
:root {
  /* Paper */
  --p-paper:    #FBFBFC;  /* the surface everything sits on */
  --p-surface:  #FFFFFF;  /* raised: cards, sheets, the share frame */

  /* Ink */
  --p-ink:      #10141C;  /* verdicts, figures that matter        17.8:1 AAA */
  --p-ink-2:    #394152;  /* body, ledes                           9.9:1 AAA */
  --p-muted:    #666E85;  /* meta, labels, source tags             4.9:1 AA  */
  --p-faint:    #949CAD;  /* NON-TEXT ONLY — ticks, empty states, rules */

  /* Rules */
  --p-line:     #E4E7ED;  /* hairline between rows */
  --p-line-2:   #C9CFDA;  /* structural: table edges, frame borders */
  --p-line-3:   #868FA4;  /* interactive borders — inputs, chips    3.1:1    */

  /* Meridian — the one accent. Action, links, focus. Never decoration. */
  --p-accent:   #223FA6;  /*                                        8.7:1 AAA */
  --p-accent-w: #EBEFFB;

  /* The three states. Load-bearing — never decorative. */
  --p-open:     #14654A;  --p-open-w: #E8F3EE;  /* Open now      6.8:1 AA */
  --p-fix:      #A05A00;  --p-fix-w:  #FCF3E5;  /* Within reach  5.1:1 AA */
  --p-settled:  #5A6273;  --p-settled-w: #F0F2F5; /* Settled     5.9:1 AA */
}
```

Ratios are measured against `--p-paper`, not assumed. Every text token above passes WCAG AA at body size; `--p-ink` and `--p-ink-2` clear AAA.

### 5.2 The three states are the brand

Most products pick a palette and then find things to colour. Polaris is the reverse: **the palette is a state machine, and it is the most distinctive thing we own visually.** Three colours, three futures for a door.

| State | Colour | Says | Instruction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open now** | Green `#14654A` | Eligible today | Convert it |
| **Within reach** | Amber `#A05A00` | Closed, and fixable | Here is the lever, in hours |
| **Settled** | Grey `#5A6273` | Closed, nothing to fix | *No instruction.* That is the point |

Both closed states are desaturated relative to green on purpose — the eye must land on what is open and what is winnable before it finds what is lost. Grey for settled is a **product** decision (v2 note) that the palette exists to serve.

Green is deep and unsaturated — nearer a ledger's ink than a "success" toast. Amber is umber, not warning-yellow. Nothing in this palette is a notification colour.

### 5.3 Rules

1. **Never encode state in colour alone.** Every state carries a glyph *and* a word: `●` Open now, `◐` Within reach, `○` Settled. Roughly 8% of men have a colour vision deficiency and our audience is majority male engineering students — a red/amber/green product that leans on hue is broken for a meaningful slice of a lecture hall. This is also what makes a compressed screenshot survive.
2. **Meridian blue is for action only.** Links, primary buttons, focus rings. The moment it becomes a decorative brand wash, the student cannot tell what is tappable.
3. **One accent, full stop.** No secondary brand colour. Where a fourth colour seems needed, the answer is a rule, a weight change, or more space.
4. **Washes are backgrounds, never text.** `--p-*-w` tints only.
5. **No gradients. No coloured shadows. No glow.** Elevation is a hairline and, at most, a shadow at ≤ 8% opacity.

### 5.4 Two corrections to the flow board's palette

Measured, not eyeballed:

| Token | Was | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--p-muted` | `#6B7488` | **`#666E85`** | 4.53:1 on paper — technically AA, but drops to **4.18:1 on the tinted washes**, which fails. Since muted text sits inside settled and reach cards constantly, it fails in the place it is used most. `#666E85` gives 4.91 on paper and 4.53 on wash — passes both |
| `--p-faint` | text colour | **non-text only** | `#949CAD` is **2.67:1** — well under AA. In the flow board it currently sets `23 companies · 4 colleges · updated 2 Aug 2026`, which is *trust-bearing* text and the first thing lost to JPEG compression. There is no room to darken it without colliding with muted, so the honest resolution is that the palette's text floor is `--p-muted`, and faint is for rules, tick marks and empty states. **Move that line to `--p-muted`** |
| `--p-line-3` | *(new)* | **`#868FA4`** | WCAG 1.4.11 wants 3:1 on the boundary of an interactive component. `--p-line-2` at 1.51:1 is fine for a table rule but too faint for an input border. `#868FA4` reaches 3.13:1 and keeps the cool hue |

## 6. Typography

### 6.1 Three voices, one rule

| Voice | Face | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **Verdict** | Display serif | The single declarative sentence per screen. Nothing else |
| **Body** | System sans | Ledes, labels, buttons, chrome. Deliberately neutral |
| **Figure** | Monospace, tabular | Every number, cutoff, date, count and source tag |

The rule that makes this a system rather than three fonts:

> **If it came from the database, it is set in the Figure face.** If the model wrote it, it is Body. If it is the one thing the screen exists to say, it is Verdict.

So typography encodes provenance. A student can *see* which parts are retrieved fact and which are prose — §8's Hard Rule 2 made visible, and the cheapest possible answer to "isn't this just ChatGPT?"

Verdict is rationed. One per screen. A serif used for every heading is a magazine; a serif used once is a statement.

### 6.2 Faces, against the performance budget

**Phase 0 ships zero webfonts on the ledger path.** The `/check → /ledger` route is the most valuable and most fragile moment in the funnel (< 3s on mid-range Android, 4G) and a font swap there is a visible flash for no gain. Keep the flow board's system stacks:

```css
--font-display: "Iowan Old Style", Charter, Cambria, Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif;
--font-ui:      system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI Variable Text", "Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif;
--font-data:    ui-monospace, "Cascadia Mono", "SF Mono", "Roboto Mono", Consolas, monospace;
```

Most of our users land on Roboto and a Noto or Droid serif. That is acceptable: **the system's character lives in the rationing, the rules and the figures, not in owning a typeface.**

When a face is affordable — marketing pages, the wordmark, `/for-colleges`, the TPO PDF — subset it and load it *only there*:

| Slot | Recommendation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Newsreader** or **Instrument Serif** | Both OFL. Editorial rather than literary; Instrument Serif has more edge at large sizes |
| Figure | **IBM Plex Mono** | Honest and technical without the developer-tool costume. True tabular figures |
| Body | Leave as system-ui | If ever forced: **Public Sans** or **IBM Plex Sans**. Not Inter — it is the default of the thing we are trying not to look like |

### 6.3 Scale

From the flow board, ratified. Verdict tracks at `-0.018em`, line-height 1.16, `text-wrap: balance`.

| Token | Size | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `v-xl` | 30px | The shock. Ledger, audit, re-shock |
| `v-lg` | 24px | Screen verdict |
| `v-md` | 19px | Section verdict |
| lede | 14px / 1.55 | One sentence. Ideally zero |
| body | 14px | Default |
| tiny | 12px | Meta. **The floor for content** — source tags, dates, counts, context lines |
| eyebrow | 10.5px, `.1em`, upper, mono | **Labels only.** See the carve-out below |

> **The 10.5px carve-out.** Uppercase mono tracked at `.1em` stays legible below the 12px floor where sentence-case would not, so *labels* may sit at 10.5px — "OPEN NOW", "YOUR TRAJECTORY", "SOMEONE LIKE YOU". A label names a region the reader is already looking at. The moment an element carries information that appears nowhere else — a source tag, a date, a count — it is content, and content is 12px. Building the demo (`design/brand-demo.html`) is what surfaced this: the source tag had been drawn at 11px, which is exactly the element §8.3 calls close to the most important in the system.

### 6.4 Numerals

1. **`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on every figure.** Columns must align; a jittering readiness score reads as an animation rather than a measurement.
2. **Never round a cutoff.** 63.8%, not "about 64%." The precision *is* the credibility.
3. **Units close and small.** `340` at figure weight, `hours` at muted.
4. **Indian numbering where money appears.** ₹1,499 · 12 LPA · lakh. Never `$` and never `1,499.00`.
5. **A figure never appears without its provenance** when it came from the registry. See §7.3.

## 7. The marks

### 7.1 Primary — **The Fix**

A sight mark, not a star: a fixed point with four ticks, the northern one longer and breaking out further than the rest. A surveyor's benchmark, a reticle, a position plotted on a chart.

The asymmetric north tick is the whole design. It encodes the meridian, it makes the mark legibly *drawn* rather than defaulted, and — critically — it keeps us clear of the four-point sparkle now stamped on every AI feature in the world (§1.4).

```svg
<svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" width="24" height="24" fill="none"
     stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.6" stroke-linecap="round"
     role="img" aria-label="Polaris">
  <circle cx="12" cy="12" r="2.4" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/>
  <path d="M12 1.5V7"/>      <!-- meridian: longest, breaks the ring -->
  <path d="M12 17v3.5"/>
  <path d="M3.5 12H7"/>
  <path d="M17 12h3.5"/>
</svg>
```

Renders in `currentColor` — ink on paper, paper on Meridian, never two-tone.

### 7.2 Secondary — **The Ledger**

Four bars at uneven heights, echoing the 23-tick counter. Already specified as the Ledger tab icon in the flow board's v3 note; promoted here to the app icon and favicon, because at 32px a bar chart survives where a reticle turns to mush.

```svg
<svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" width="24" height="24" fill="currentColor"
     role="img" aria-label="Ledger">
  <rect x="3"    y="9"  width="3" height="12" rx="1.5"/>
  <rect x="8.5"  y="5"  width="3" height="16" rx="1.5"/>
  <rect x="14"   y="12" width="3" height="9"  rx="1.5"/>
  <rect x="19.5" y="15" width="3" height="6"  rx="1.5"/>
</svg>
```

### 7.3 Wordmark

**Polaris** — Display face, weight 500, tracking `-0.018em`, sentence case. Never all-caps, never lowercase, never letterspaced open.

The flow board's header lockup is already correct and becomes the standard: wordmark in Display, with an optional mono eyebrow beside or beneath it at 10.5px / `.1em` / uppercase / muted.

```
Polaris   FOR VTU 3RD-YEARS · BENGALURU
```

**Lockups:** horizontal (mark + wordmark, gap = 0.6× cap height, baselines aligned) · stacked (mark centred above, gap = 1× cap height) · wordmark alone — the default in-product · mark alone for favicon, app icon and the share-card corner.

### 7.4 Rules

- **Clear space:** the length of the north tick on every side. Nothing enters it.
- **Minimums:** mark 16px (at 16px drop the side ticks — dot and meridian only); wordmark 72px wide.
- **Colour:** Ink, Meridian, or paper knocked out of Meridian. Nothing else.
- **Misuse:** do not rotate · do not add rays, glow or a starburst · do not gradient the dot · do not place on photography · do not outline the wordmark · do not set the wordmark in the Body face · never animate the mark as a twinkle.

## 8. Signature components

A brand needs one thing that is unmistakably its own before it needs a logo. Ours is not the mark — it is the row.

### 8.1 The ruled row

The atom of the product and the thing that gets screenshotted.

```
● Open now      TCS NQT          6.4 ≥ 6.0 · 0 backlogs      [TCS 2026 criteria]
◐ Within reach  Wipro Elite      2 active backlogs → 0       [Wipro 2026 criteria]
○ Settled       Infosys          12th 63.8% vs 65%           [Infosys 2026 criteria]
```

State glyph and label at the left, entity in Body, **the arithmetic in Figures**, source tag at the right. Hairline `--p-line` between rows, no card, no shadow. Repeated twenty-three times, ruled and aligned, this *is* the brand — and no wrapper can produce it, because it needs a registry.

### 8.2 The tick counter

Twenty-three marks, nine filled. Appears on `/today`, the share card and, as four bars, in the nav. It is the readiness and eligibility idea in a form that is legible at 12px and at thumbnail size in a group chat. Never a donut chart. Never a percentage ring.

### 8.3 The source tag

`[Wipro 2026 criteria]` — mono, muted, bracketed, **12px minimum**, always adjacent to the figure it justifies.

This is the smallest element in the system and close to the most important. It is §12.2 made visible, it is what makes an unwelcome number survive being doubted, and it is the single clearest visual difference between us and a chat answer. **A retrieved figure never ships without one.**

### 8.4 The share card — `/r/[slug]`

The most-seen brand surface we will ever have, and the one an art director never sees. Designed under §9's rules.

Contains: the count as the hero (`9 / 23`) · the tick counter · at most three ruled rows, mixed states · college + branch + year, no name, no exact CGPA (`platform.md` §6 Q5) · the wordmark, bottom-left, always · a stated date.

Constraints: readable at 240px wide · legible cropped to the top half · no text under 12px · nothing essential in the outer 8% · flat colour only, since gradients band badly under WhatsApp's re-encode.

## 9. Designed for the screenshot

§2.3 makes this the primary channel, so it gets rules rather than good intentions. Every one of these is a consequence of an image being re-encoded, resized, and viewed on a stranger's phone.

1. **12px is the floor for content.** The only exception is the 10.5px uppercase mono label (§6.3) — and a label never carries information that appears nowhere else.
2. **Nothing meaningful at `--p-faint`.** Low-contrast grey is the first casualty of JPEG.
3. **The wordmark is in frame on every shareable surface** — ledger, audit, re-shock, share card. An unattributed screenshot is free reach we did not collect.
4. **Nothing essential in the outer 8%.** People crop badly and thumbnails clip.
5. **State reads without colour** (§5.3) — glyph and word, so it survives a screenshot of a screenshot.
6. **Flat fills only.** Gradients band; hairlines below 1px vanish.
7. **A date on every artefact.** "Updated 2 Aug 2026" is what makes a forwarded image credible in November.

> Design review question for any new surface: *if someone crops this to a square and sends it to three friends, does it still make the argument — and can they tell who made it?*

## 10. Motion

Restraint is the position, not a budget constraint.

- Transitions 120–180ms, ease-out. Nothing eases in.
- **The re-shock — seven rows flipping settled→open — gets a 180ms crossfade per row, staggered 40ms, and the counter counts.** Nothing else. No confetti, no sound, no burst. The event is one of the most significant things that will happen to this student this semester, and treating it like a mobile game would cheapen it. The fact carries it.
- The countdown loading state (§10.2) is the one place motion does real work: numbers count up, then hold.
- Never animate the mark. It does not move — that is the entire idea.
- Honour `prefers-reduced-motion` everywhere; the flow board already does.

## 12. Open decisions

## 13. Is this Polaris?

Six questions. A no on any of them is a rewrite, not a tweak.

1. Could this sentence have been written without our database? *(If yes, it is a wrapper. §4)*
2. Does every retrieved figure carry its source tag?
3. Is the headline under seven words, with no hedges?
4. Does every closed door say either what fixes it, in hours, or nothing at all?
5. Cropped to a square and forwarded to three friends — does it still argue, and is the wordmark in frame?
6. Would it still look right if the news it delivered were bad? *(The form must not flatter what the content says.)*
